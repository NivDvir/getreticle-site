What it is

A non-invasive AI layer between you and your machine — a second pair of eyes for your screen. It closes the gap between what you see and what's actually there — and marks up what it finds on a layer laid over the content, like acetate, never touching what's underneath. It's loyal to you by construction: structurally confined to its own layer, it runs on your machine, reads only the pixels you see — never an app's internals — answers only in its own overlay, and can do only what you could do yourself, in plain sight. And it perceives your screen the way you do, on any app — so it works where tools that read the code underneath come up empty.

Where it reaches that others can't

Native controls, canvas-drawn UIs, remote/VDI desktops — the surfaces where the accessibility tree carries no semantics: it has the words but can't say what is actionable. Reticle works from the pixels and your point of view, so it perceives those surfaces the way you do. It complements the assistive tools you already use — it doesn't replace them — making the parts they can't say are actionable perceivable alongside them.
